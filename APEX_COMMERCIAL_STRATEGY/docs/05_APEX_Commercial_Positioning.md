# APEX Commercial Positioning

_Inspironics APEX · Commercial Strategy · November 2026. Internal sales enablement._

> Readiness: capabilities marked LIVE are built and tested; none is yet proven in a customer production environment. No customer results, ROI, certifications or prices are claimed.


## Positioning statement
> For engineering and technology-services organisations whose know-how lives in playbooks, decks and blueprints, APEX is a self-hosted knowledge-to-capability platform that turns scattered material into a searchable portfolio, a ready sales library and verified team capability — behind one secure front door.

## The questions answered
- **What exactly are we selling?** An annual, self-hosted platform licence (front door and Core, plus modules) with an implementation package and a paid pilot as the entry point.
- **Who should buy it?** Mid-sized engineering, technology-services and solution-integrator firms (about 200–5,000 people) that already store their know-how in Dropbox.
- **Why should they buy it?**
  - Faster, consistent pre-sales: the right deck and blueprint, found and presented in minutes.
  - Evidence of capability: who completed which playbook, with certificates anyone can verify.
  - Currency: people re-certify automatically when a playbook changes.
  - Governance: one sign-in, scoped roles, audit, and data that never leaves the organisation.
- **Which business problems justify the purchase?**
  - Knowledge scattered across folders: Blueprints, decks and playbooks sit in shared folders: hard to find, hard to present, and no one knows what is used.
  - Playbooks nobody learns from: Engineering playbooks are written once and rarely read; there is no proof of who knows them or when they went out of date.
  - Too many tools, too many logins: Each tool has its own address and password; access, roles and audit are inconsistent and hard to govern.
- **What makes APEX commercially valuable?**
  - Self-hosted: data stays on the customer’s servers and in its own Dropbox; files are proxied, never linked.
  - Playbook-to-certificate pipeline with traceability from every lesson back to its source chapter.
  - Assessment integrity: grading on the server, answers never reach the browser, certificates publicly verifiable.
  - AI drafts, people decide: AI is optional, human-approved, with daily and monthly spend caps.
  - One governed identity: scoped roles, organisations and an audit trail across products.
- **How do we differentiate it from the alternatives?**

| Alternative | APEX position |
|---|---|
| Generic LMS | APEX builds courses from your own playbooks and keeps them current; an LMS needs content made for it. |
| File shares / intranet | APEX makes material searchable, presentable and measured, without moving it out of Dropbox. |
| Sales enablement SaaS | APEX is self-hosted and joins sales content with engineering capability in one front door. |
| Building in-house | APEX is built and tested today; a pilot runs in weeks rather than a build taking quarters. |

- **What evidence must we prepare before making ROI claims?**
  - Baseline before the pilot: time to find a deck, playbook completion, time to onboard a new engineer.
  - Pilot measurements from the customer’s own usage analytics and completion records.
  - A signed pilot report the customer agrees can be quoted.
  - Security review outcome and the customer’s hosting decision.

## What is on the roadmap (say so plainly)
- Hosted deployment with Postgres and HTTPS
- Enterprise single sign-on (SAML/OIDC)
- Live Dropbox connection in all products
- Email delivery for invites and reminders
- Privacy self-service: data export and erasure

## Pricing framework (no prices are set)

| Model | How it applies to APEX |
|---|---|
| Organisation licence (annual) | Recommended core: one self-hosted install per organisation. |
| Modular pricing | Front Door & Core always included; Showcase, SlidesVault, Academia as modules. |
| User bands | Tiers by named users (for example up to 250, 1,000, 5,000) rather than per-seat billing. |
| Implementation package | Installation, Dropbox connection, first playbooks converted, admin training. |
| Content conversion service | Playbook-to-course conversion and review as a fixed-scope service. |
| Support & updates tier | Standard and priority support with update entitlement. |
| AI usage | Customer’s own AI keys; APEX enforces budgets. No AI margin assumed. |

**Assessment of the requested models:**
- *Enterprise subscription / annual licensing:* recommended as an annual self-hosted organisation licence. A hosted subscription becomes possible only after hosted deployment is ready.
- *Modular pricing:* fits naturally, because the front door and Core are always included and Showcase, SlidesVault and Academia are modules.
- *Organisation-based vs user-based:* organisation licence with user bands. Pure per-seat billing does not fit a self-hosted install.
- *Implementation and support packages:* required. Installation, Dropbox connection, first content conversion and admin training.
- *Enterprise customisation:* offer as scoped professional services, not as product promises.

**Information needed to set prices:**
- Cost to deliver an install and support it per year
- Pilot findings on value per user and per module
- Competitor and alternative price points in target markets
- Customer budget owners and procurement thresholds
- Hosting choice (customer-hosted or managed) once hosting is ready
